I was adopted at birth, but it wasn't something that occurred to me much as a kid. I spent a lot of time with my Grandma, aunt, uncles and cousins, and I never recall feeling different from them. We celebrated every birthday, got together at all of the holidays and it was a tight-nit group and it felt so warm and amazing to me as a child.

After the death of my mother and uncle, my cousin announced that she was engaged to someone that made her feel whole. My aunt, (other uncle) and cousins decided that the conflict with their [[Religion]] made it impossible for them to attend my cousin's wedding. I attended with my partner and kids as the only Butskos and I still can't imagine how painful it was for my cousin, who, going on ten years later, is still completely severed from that side family. 

My aunt and uncle have always been there for me, and I haven't quite reconciled my take on their decision not to attend my cousin's wedding. I think that in an open society we have to try to not cut people off for expressing their beliefs, but what about when that expression is itself cutting them off from other people for reasons that don't make any sense to me?
