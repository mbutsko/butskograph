I don't believe in objective [[Free Will]], but I do believe that we're better off acting as though we're responsible for how our actions impact others. 

Whether genetics (COMT) or just personality, I have a tendency to become easily and intensely agitated. When I lose my cool to the point of needing to physically let it out, it tends to be directed at inanimate objects (as a kid, I remember smashing an SNES remote against the CRT TV screen). About once a year I lose my cool with or in front of my family. Twice I can remember slamming dinner plates, a couple times a slammed door. Only once (after 6th grade?) have a laid a hand on another person out of anger, and its my most regretful moment. 

On a hike in Canada with Jessica, Adeline and Madelyn, we were being bothered by a bee. Maddie gets very anxious about bees and she was walking ahead of me. I was tired (a variety of factors meant I was pretty sleep deprived) and I was really annoyed that she just couldn't keep going and just ignore the bee. I totally understand what its like to be scared of something in the moment like that and I wish that I had been (and generally could always be) understanding and empathetic, but instead I was just annoyed. I said twice that if she would just pick up the pace we'd get past the bee, but she couldn't hear me. I lost my cool, and I put my hands on her back and pushed her; I wasn't trying to knock her down, I just wanted to make her walk faster or run so that we could be done but in that moment I pushed too hard (not that any amount of hard was ok) and Maddie tumbled forward and hit her knee on a gnarly root in the ground. I took the time to explain all of this to her and make it clear that I was really sorry but that being sorry doesn't excuse the behavior or make it ok.

That memory stands out to me as a demon in my past. I actively work on handling those situations more appropriately than I did in that moment, but I also try not to beat myself up about it.
