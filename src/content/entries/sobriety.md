I hastily wrote the following about my experience with addiction when I stopped drinking In October of 2022. It's a rough cut to remind me of why I needed to stop drinking at that point in my life-- short bursts of memories of how addiction got out of hand to remind me of why I needed to stop drinking. It's raw and unedited. I've left it as is. Since this was written I've struggled with [[Compulsive, Disordered Eating]], so that article is sort of a continuation of this story. 

Periodically (like a few times a year), I will have a few drinks in the course of a week. It no longer offers the same mental reward, and it just kinda makes me feel crummy. 
 
 -- Compiled from January 6 - October 29, 2022 

The day that I learned that the same songs repeated every three hours on Top 40 radio stations was a glorious lazy Saturday. Ohio state played football in the afternoon and all felt right with the world. I believe I eight nine glazed donuts that day. Nothing seemed wrong with that at the time. It didn’t seem strange that my mom bought a dozen glazed donuts (presumably not meant for a single day), nor that I ate the majority of them at eight or nine years old.

In chronological order, I have been addicted to food, cigarettes, cough medicine and alcohol over the course of my life. Maybe you could add exercise and protein power to that list, but there are a whole slew of addictive things that I have tried that haven’t interested me, or that I have managed to maintain a great distance from. 

I remember the first time I stole a cigarette from my mom. She kept them in a faux leather case, not like a fancy case from a film noir, but a cheap coin purse enlargened to hold soft packs of Now Menthol 100’s and a mini bic lighter. I was probably in the sixth grade when that happened. I broke it in half because I was curious what was in it, and I remember that the smell offended me and that I threw it away. I don’t specifically recall the next time that I swiped one from that case, but by 8th grade I had discovered that I could take entire packs from the carton and for years I never even heard a hint from my mom that she suspected me of (or, upon reflection, cared enough to call me out for) any wrongdoing. I had a cigarette on the way to school, one on the way home, and usually would find an excuse to sneak out in the evening to a spot in the woods in the park near our house to have one or two more.  
  
By the time she finally stumbled upon me while I was smoking at 16 (by which time I had moved to the garage because it was just closer and less cold in the winter), she said that she wasn’t surprised and had known for some time.  
  
I hid my smoking from lots of people until I finally decided at 28 years of age that I had had enough. I took up running and haven’t had a single puff of a cigarette in ten years. Over the course of that time there were periods when I would have more than a pack a day, and some periods where I would go several days without and then several days with, on and off, beating myself up for lacking the self control to stop or the dignity just to be honest with people about it.

The first time a friend of mine convinced me to take 8 Coricidan HBP pills, I had no idea what DXM was, nor that I was taking a fairly massive dose of a serotonin reuptake inhibitor. For the next two summers, and every weekend in between, I would take my regular dose every chance that I got. It was prior to any reasonable restrictions, and it was easy enough to cycle between all of the local grocery and drug stores. The high lasted 4 hours; it was completely dissociative, and quite like I have pieced together from pop culture that heroin must be. We assumed that because of the “High Blood Pressure” suffix that it was just lowering our blood pressure making us feel kind kind of chill. This was, at 14 (9th grade) my third addiction, except that if you’d asked me then, I’d have no idea.

I finally stopped taking cough medicine because it got old, and as our tight knit circle of friends started to disband during my senior year of high school. Unfortunately, one of my friends continued regular use of pure DXM among some of them has a 100% correlation with a pretty sucky life, so I am glad that I escaped.

Then I started drinking.

On my 21st birthday, I had my first drink of red wine, at a party at my house. I don’t recall having more than two glasses, and while I enjoyed it, it was not out of control.

11 months later, I had my first night of excess. I recall buying a mini-keg of Oberon ale for a house party. There was also a giant punch bowl. I recall reciting the Euthyphro plotline to Nick, then being passed out in a puddle of guacamole vomit on the bathroom floor, with Stephen trying to make sure I wasn’t going to die.

During a breakup, I had my first (relatively rare) experience of intentionally drinking too much (a six-pack of Guinness as quickly as I could) while alone in my apartment (with my roomate Nick, who wasn't at home at the time). I drank it alone. I don’t recall anything really bad happening as a result.

Shortly after (still reeling from the breakup) at a movie theatre coworker's apartment. I Had way too much Cheap vodka from her fridge. I did summer-sults on her floor and broke a cd rack. Was the most awkward wake up and creep out at 6:00am ever. I felt like a total ass (despite remembering a quiet, pretty sunrise at Goodale park).

I recall being pulled over for driving through a redlight, after drinking, by a Police officer on Neil Ave on the night of the Michigan game. I apologized and escaped, but surely I should not have.

I recall many fires over my 20's having two to five beers in an evening at my mom’s house, sometimes more. There was rarely if ever any driving afterward.

I remember the feeling of being queasy drinking beers at the Rose and Thistle with the The Graham School staff. It felt good to be a part of a group.

I was visiting some friends in NYC. The evening started with Kingfishers at an upscale Indian restaurant and ended with Vodka tonics in some nondescript dark bar. Josh gave me money for a cab, but think I pocketed it and walked home. 

While Ada's mom was pregnant with her, I gave up drinking entirely. After Ada was born, I don’t recall ever drinking too much all throughout my divorce. I always had beer in the fridge but don’t recall it ever going too far.

Then I met Jess. For the first years of our relationship we actually didn't drink all that much. Our wedding night, however, was a festivus of excess drinking. It started at 3:00 with shots of bourbon, and ended with a 45 minute drive after way too much to drink. I didn't pass out or black out that night, but I definitely should not have driven.

A year or so later, Jess and I drove home from a friend's wedding – we dodged cops trying to get gas on state route 23. It's somewhat of a surprise we amde it.

PillarCon. At my first PillarCon, a company get together, there was a house party where shots of Fireball whisky were going all night. I came back to the hotel room and Jess said I smelled like Vodka.

A moment in a friend's basement on New Year's eve talking about my that being my first alcohol free January. I said I had had a bottle of bourbon in a week. The friend cringed. Looking back now that seems so tame.

I went alone to Portland for a tech conference. On the second night – I had way too much, couldn’t sleep and vomited after laying in bed in a rock and roll hotel room, then rode around on a bike share bike feeling sorry for myself. I drank just as much the next night. On the final night of that trip, I had a beer with dinner, rode my bikeshare bike towards the airport, stopped for another two beers. I ditch the bike, walked for a while and stop for dinner. Had another beer. A long walk, lost my eye glasses, finally getting to airport a hot mess. I had a lot of drinks at the airport bar with the golf pro. I then got on my red eye. I remember vomiting, sitting back down with vomit still on my shirt and passing out for the 6 hours of the flight and being nudged awake in the morning Newark, NJ (where I got in a fight a fight with a TSA employee).

The Root Christmas Party -- I recall very little of the evening. Stealing drink tokens and getting very drunk. Jess and I Uber’d home, barely remember seeing babysitter. In the picture, I look happy.
    
Camping with my daughters: I stopped to get a man can of Miller Lite and put it in a Kleen Kanteen to chug in the tent because I was afraid I wouldn't be able to sleep.

During our RV camping trip, riding to the gas station in the town to make sure we had an extra little amount of cheap beer and hard iced tea.

Day after our RV road trip. Jess and I downed a bottle of vodka in 30 minutes, both completely wrecked in puddles of vomit. Probably should have died. Jess. called our neighbor Carrie to come over after she couldn’t find me because I was in a puddle of vomit on the bathroom floor. Worst night of drinking ever.
  
The last half of 2021, especially after my trip on the bourbon trail. Lots of whiskey – in one particular week I believe I purchased three bottles. At home it was a mix of bottles or boxes of red wine, usually some peanut butter whiskey, and always had two kinds of beer (Miller Lite and something more delicious). Near the end, I started sneaking out to the garage to chug white claw.

One evening, I had a few rounds of peanut butter whiskey. The family was supposed to go to Whit’s and look at Christmas lights. Jess started feeling really sick, and in a frustrated attempt to entertain the kids I put them in the car and drove. I was certainly over the limit, and I endangered my kids. I didn’t get pulled over and drove all through UA so that they could see the lights. 

I managed to completely stop drinking from 1/1 - 3/20 of 2021 and I felt great about it. I really improved my outlook, participated in an online community around being sober, loved Andy Ramage (1 year no beer) on Instagram.

In the Spring of 2021 I decided I no longer needed to abstain after watching the episode of Ted Lasso where he talks about how the hard thing to do is face having a healthy relationship with things that give you joy but tend to control you. I wish I could say that I succeeded in forming a healthy relationship with Alcohol, but I didn’t. Within a few weeks I had too much to drink and Jess had to spend all day Sunday with the kids because I was thoroughly hungover. We called it food poisoning and I was humiliated. I managed to not drink only that one single day and started again by Monday:

Oddly enough, I know that I _will_ regain control on January 1st again.  I take every January+ off. I feel great. I lose some weight. I sleep better. I post to instagram. Then I find some reasonable reason that I should be able to maintain a reasonable level of drinking (COVID, Ted Lasso), and then I start the whole cycle over again.

> Honestly, if I could figure out the right mentality and support structure, I probably wouldn’t even be leaving Root (it’s exactly a year since I left HOVER, same circumstance.)This time of year I feel desperate for change.

> This week was my last at Root and I had more than half a bottle of flavored Whiskey every night. I’ve managed to only wake up hot and bothered for a stressful hour every other night or so. I gotta stop, so I am down to my last drink and am looking forward to taking another break. I am also scared, but I can even quite articulate why.

> “I am an alcoholic. I convinced myself based on a Ted Lasso speech back in March after ~70 days of sobriety that I should just be able to enjoy a drink on occasion when appropriate. Turns out that I just can’t do that. I have learned how to live with my day revolving around 2-5 drinks every single evening since then. It’s deplorable. I can still get out of bed every day at 5:45 to get to the gym. I don’t have any DUIs. I just live a pretty solemn existence where once I am in the house for the evening I sedate myself. I read to my kids at bedtime. I don’t pass out. I watch Ryan Reynolds failing at Wrexham FC with Jess and then I sleep for 5 hours like a rock. Then I wake up and spend one or two miserable hours if insatiable guilt at 3:00am wishing I could just mix up my routine to regain my control.

By the time I got COVID for the first time in July of 2021 I was back to desperately drinking everyday. I think I somehow figured out how to drink everyday even through having COVID.

On our anniversary trip with Jess to Boulder, I had lots of beer and Peanut butter whiskey; was tipsy at least all day every day. In some ways it was fun, but the sadness of it all hit me really hard in the mornings.

Vacationed with Maddie's friend's family to New River Gorge and it was a bottle of Cabernet every day and a can of beer.

Now it’s October 29 and I feel pretty bad about drinking and pretty motivated to stop again. I finally confessed the severity of my feelings to Nick (who said he had tried wine again and once again gave it up because it missed with his sleep) and Chris.
