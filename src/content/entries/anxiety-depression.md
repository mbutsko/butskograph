I first remember having a panic attack (for lack of a more fitting name),  at the age of 16 (almost 17) in a very social situation. In the years that followed, I would have frequent, crippling episodes like this. I fell into depression, and eventually tried Zoloft for a period of a few months. I don't really remember anything specifically lifting me out of it, but I made the transition to college. Now I still have these episodes a few time a year. It isn't at all the same as how other people describe panic attacks -- I do feel overwhelmed, but rather than sweaty and heart racing, I just kind of get tunnel vision and feel very awkward and just kinda want to go to sleep. 

My social anxiety has increased since I (mostly) dealt with [[Sobriety & Addiction]]. 

Depression isn't at all binary, but I have had rather acute periods of depression over the years. Over time, this has mellowed into a more continuous '3/10' feeling. The most fitting term is Anhedonia (the inability to feel pleasure or joy from things you usually like). I still get up at the same time every morning, do some [[Calisthenics]], [[Running]] and be the first person in the office. I am able to spend time writing things down for this site. I go to all of my kids' sporting events.  

Instead, for me, it shows up as:
* having no interest in celebrations
* extreme minimalism -- avoidance of receiving gifts or buying anything for myself
* feeling like any kind of vacation isn't worth the stress
* avoiding company social gatherings
* only having hobbies that serve some other end (running, cycling, walking, etc.)
* walking alone without headphones or starting off into space ruminating about things that don't matter

I have tried talk therapy. It feels productive in the moment but never resulted in any sustained changes (4 therapists over 20 years). I was prescribed Fluvoxamine (an SSRI) in the Spring of 2025, but immediately began having intense episodes where I would sob uncontrollably (more like traditional panic attack than the episodes I started having at 17), so I gave up on that after two months.
