The fitness mainstream seems certain that BMI is a bad measure for health because it is not the same as body composition. It's popular right now to advocate unboundedly for _more muscle_, and that more muscle requires _more protein_ To my knowledge, there isn't any epidemiology supporting humans being 225 pounds of solid muscle, because until recently we didn't have a lot of Cross Fitters eating Paleo. 

I'm not saying that you can't be healthy and live a long time on 1g protein/pound of bodyweight, but we definitely don't have any longitudinal data saying that doing that for a whole life of 90 years will help you to live longer, and we have a lot of bodybuilders falling over dead in their 40s or 50s, as well as a shit ton of mouse data suggesting that you live quite a bit longer with a lower metabolism _at a lower bodyweight independent of body composition_. 

Frailty in old age is a concern, so the is to combat it by building a reserve of muscle mass to give you a protective cushion in old age. My $0.02: if you do manage to break a hip, get cancer, or even pneumonia, all of that excess muscle mass will deplete itself in weeks anyway, so the odds that carrying 40 pounds of extra body mass for 40 years will have been enough to make a difference when that happens is quite slim. The amount of strength required to prevent a fall and breaking a hip is actually _quite small_ and does not require deadlifting 2x bodyweight.

On the flip-side, the increased heart rate and metabolism that comes along with carrying extra muscle mass is likely to speed up aging. I suspect within 15 years we'll figure out that this (like ultra low-fat diets refined carbs, Adkins diet, etc.) protein/muscle obsession has a downside was was just another fad with downsides we easily should have seen coming. Having strength important, but the amount of protein, strength training and muscle _far exceeds_ what we need to prevent frailty.

I'll stick with enough muscle for [[Calisthenics]]
